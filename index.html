Ironing Calibration Converter

For the Customizable Ironing Calibration on MakerWorld. Drop in your 3MF and get it back with every pad's ironing flow and speed already set.

1. Drop your 3MF

Your file stays on your computer. Nothing is uploaded.

2. Check your pads

Seen from above, with the front of the plate at the bottom.

3. Save the new 3MF

Advanced settings
  • Ironing type: Topmost surface only, so the label strips aren't ironed.
  • Pattern: Rectilinear.
  • Fixed ironing angle: on, so every pad is ironed in the same direction.

These apply to every pad. Use the same values when you set up ironing with your result.

4. Slice and print

  1. Open the new 3MF and pick your printer, filament and process as usual.
  2. Turn off ironing overrides in your filament settings (Setting Overrides → Ironing). If they're ticked, every pad irons the same.
  3. Slice and print, then pick the smoothest pad. Its flow and speed are engraved on it.

Ironing line spacing

Default 0.15 mm (0.4 mm nozzle)

  • The distance between ironing passes. Keep it at or below the nozzle diameter.
  • Tighter is smoother but slower: 0.10 makes ironing take about 1.5× as long as 0.15.
  • 0.2 mm nozzle: start around 0.08.

Ironing inset

Default 0.21 mm

  • How far in from the edge of the top surface the ironing stops.
  • 0.21 is half a 0.42 mm line, so ironing stops just inside the outer wall. At 0, OrcaSlicer uses half the nozzle diameter.
  • Raise it if ironed edges get a raised lip or blobs. Lower it if you see a rough un-ironed border.

Ironing angle

Default 0°, fixed (OrcaSlicer only)

  • The direction of the ironing lines. It's fixed, so every pad is ironed the same way.
  • With the usual 45° top surface lines, use 0° or 90° so ironing crosses them.
  • If your top surface lines run at another angle, pick one that crosses them.

How it works: each pad has a tiny code under its label strip that holds its flow and speed. It's cut only 0.02 mm into the bottom, so it never prints and stays clear of the ironed test area. This page reads it from the file.
